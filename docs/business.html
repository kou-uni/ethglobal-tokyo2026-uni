We are never paid out of
the seller’s money.

Their payment is a direct transfer to them. We are not a destination on it and we never hold it. What we charge for is the work we did — and it is charged the way the protocol says to charge something smaller than gas.

⚠ None of this is implemented. Yohaku takes nothing today. This page is the design, and the repository says so everywhere it comes up. The full version, including where it rubs against our own principles →

WHERE THE MONEY GOES

1
The agent pays the person. Directly. An exact transfer to their address. We are not on it.
DONE · ON CHAIN
2
We take a voucher, not a cut. One per decision — gas-sized, and signed by the agent if it chooses to.
DESIGNED
3
Redeemed later, in one transaction. Which is what batch-settlement exists for: “gas fees exceed the value of individual requests.”
DESIGNED

WHY PER DECISION, AND NOT A SHARE

THE INCENTIVEA share would grow when we interrupt herTake a percentage and an expensive request becomes worth escalating. We would rather not have that incentive than promise to resist it.
THE UNITTen rules, a bundle, a cap, a personhood checkThat is the work, and it is the same work whether the request was worth 80 or 6,000. A refusal costs us the same, so it costs the same.
THE ALIGNMENTA fee she notices closes the counterAnd a closed counter pays us nothing. Keeping it invisible is not generosity — it is the only version that works.

HOW IT GROWS

StageChargedWhy
SmallNothingCharging for something nobody uses teaches you nothing
TrafficPer decision handledPriced against the thing being bought: how many times she was not asked
Learned filteringA little moreFewer interruptions, and a share of what that saved. Not on day one — we measured thirty nights and the mornings only got quieter in week four

AND THE PART THAT COMPOUNDS

CONTRIBUTEThe shape of your refusals Not what you refused. What kind of asking you tend to refuse. Opt-in, and you can leave.
BECOMESSomeone else’s first day A person who opens a counter tomorrow is quiet from the start, because other people’s judgement is already in the defaults.
AND RETURNSPaid back, per use Each time it becomes a default, the contributor gets some of the fee. The platform gets cheaper as it gets better.
⚠ This is the part that rubs against us. We said the scarce thing is a person’s judgement — so asking for it back needs conditions, set before the money: opt-in only, patterns not contents, leavable, and never a summarised portrait of a person. If that stays vague, this section should not ship at all.
