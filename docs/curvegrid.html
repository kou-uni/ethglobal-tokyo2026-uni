THE HYPOTHESIS WE WOULD LIKE CORRECTED

Our guess: the hard problem in agent finance is not moving money, it is deciding which movements a person should ever see. An agent that works around the clock spends and earns around the clock, and the number that decides whether a team can live with it is not the amount — it is how many times a day someone has to look. We built that half-step. We did not build execution, and we are not claiming it. If this is not where your next layer needs help, the most useful thing you can do today is say so.

What we are going on is your public talk, where NEO was described as holding digital assets inside treasury operations and deciding whether an AI-proposed action may proceed — approve, sign, execute, record. We treat that as a design that was described, not as a list of shipped features, and we have written that distinction down rather than assuming in your favour.

THREE THINGS WE WOULD LIKE TO ASK

1

What should cross the line, and when?

This is the one we most want answered. The four fields we would send are a guess at your needs, not a specification.

  • Synchronous, or after the fact? Blocking the movement makes us part of your latency; a signed record afterwards makes us part of your audit.
  • Do you want the refusals too? What you stopped is the only evidence a control is working, and nobody keeps it — but it doubles what you ingest.
  • How much identity does a movement need? We can say an orb-level credential was verified at 07:14, bound to this request, without ever holding who they are.
  • Whose policy wins when they disagree, and who has to be told that it happened?
  • What is your unit of policy? Ours is the person, then the category, then the counterparty. If yours is the agent or the wallet, that mapping is the integration.
  • What are we not sending that you would want?
2

Are we a layer you would adopt, or a feature you would build?

Both answers are useful to us today. The second one saves us months.

  • Where does “should a person see this” live in your design now? Inside the policy engine, before it, or nowhere yet?
  • What is the smallest integration worth your time? A webhook, a signed decision record, an API we call before settling — we would rather build the small one properly.
  • What should we deliberately not do, so we stop cleanly where your layer begins?
3

Is the thesis right at all?

We are asking for a verdict here, not agreement. You have seen real systems; we have seen a simulation.

  • Are agents actually becoming the customer, or is the traffic you see still mostly human-initiated?
  • Is “less to look at” what teams want, or the opposite? We assume a person should earn automatically and only judge what needs judging. Teams often ask for more visibility.
  • At scale, what breaks first — the deciding or the moving?
  • What are you looking for? We will write the answer down in your words.

THINGS WE COULD BE WRONG ABOUT

We may be solving a problem that policy already solves

If a treasury policy engine can express “escalate to a human when X” richly enough, the decision layer is a rule set inside your product rather than a layer in front of it. We think the difference is that our unit is a person’s attention and a policy engine’s unit is a transaction — but that is an argument, not a finding.

Our volume is simulated, and we say so everywhere

Fifty-two requests a night is a generated night, reproducible from a seed, not observed customer traffic. The cap holding at two across twenty runs is a property of the code, not a market fact.

We have not run screening, personhood and settlement under load

Each is exercised, and one run does all three end to end. None of it has been run at a rate where the ordering between them matters.
