# Market

**Three numbers decide whether this is a product or a demo.** How many agents will be buying,
what they would otherwise pay, and whether the supply they currently rely on is running out.

All figures are third-party estimates with sources. Where we extrapolate, it says so.

---

## 1. The supply is running out — and this is not our claim

> **Epoch AI projects, with 80% confidence, that the stock of publicly available
> human-generated data will be fully utilised between 2026 and 2032.**
> *"Frontier models consume data faster than the internet produces it."*

That is the whole thesis in one sentence, from someone other than us. **After that stock is
exhausted, the only remaining source of human-generated data is a human, asked directly.**

And the data already has a price. Not a projected one — a paid one:

| Deal | Reported value | What is being bought |
|---|---|---|
| Google → **Reddit** | **~$60M / year** | **Things people wrote** |
| OpenAI → News Corp | ~$250M over 5 years | Journalism |
| Amazon → The New York Times | ~$20–25M / year | Journalism |
| OpenAI → Financial Times | ~$5–10M / year | Journalism |

**Reddit is the instructive one.** Sixty million dollars a year for text written by people —
**and not one cent of it reaches the people who wrote it.** That is the gap a per-person
market addresses, and it exists today, at scale, with a known price.

## 3. What they pay today

The insights industry is **~$150B in 2026** (ESOMAR-reported), up from ~$140B in 2024 —
of which the research sector proper is ~$56B.

Underneath that headline is the number that matters to us: **what one honest answer from one
person costs.**

| Audience | Cost per completed response (2026) |
|---|---|
| General population | **$2–$15** |
| With real screening criteria (n≈250) | **$40–$80** |
| Low-incidence (executives, physicians) | **$50+**, and below 5% incidence a **8–15× premium** |

**A screened study of n=400 therefore costs roughly $16,000–$32,000, and takes two to four
weeks.** That is the incumbent price of finding out what four hundred people intend to buy.

## 4. ROI for the buyer — why an agent pays at all

**Our extrapolation, from the figures above.** Stated as a range, not a point.

| | Incumbent panel | Through Yohaku |
|---|---|---|
| Per response | **$40–$80** (screened) | **~$1** (150 JPYC) |
| n=400 study | **$16,000–$32,000** | **~$400** |
| Time to result | **2–4 weeks** | **seconds to hours** |
| Who is paid | the panel company | **the person who answered** |
| Provenance | panel attestation | **proof of personhood, revocable** |

**Why the gap is this large, and why it is not a trick:** most of what a research firm sells
is study design, fieldwork management, analysis and reporting. **An agent does all of that
itself.** What it cannot do itself is be a person. So it needs to buy exactly one thing —
**a genuine human answer** — and pay for exactly that.

**This is a structural change, not a discount.** Unbundling happens when a buyer acquires
the capability that the bundle existed to supply.

> **Incumbent: two to four weeks, tens of thousands of dollars, and the respondent is paid
> a few dollars.**
> **Agent economy: the same four hundred answers, in the time it takes to ask, and the money
> goes to the four hundred people.**

## 5. Sizing, honestly

| | | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | **~$56B** | The research sector of the insights industry — the part that is *asking people things*. Not the software or reporting sectors, which agents replace rather than buy |
| **SAM** | **$4–20B by the early 2030s** | Dataset licensing reaching $22.6B (2034), plus the share of a $183B agent market spent on external data. **Both are extrapolations from other people's forecasts** |
| **SOM (first year)** | **small, and deliberately so** | One category (purchase intent), one country. At 4,324 cosmetics manufacture-and-sale licences in Japan, one study a month each and n=400, that is ~$2M/yr of demand **in a single vertical** |

**What would falsify this.** If synthetic data turns out to be an adequate substitute for
human answers, the thesis fails — and that is the single assumption worth arguing about.
Epoch's projection is about *exhaustion of supply*, not about whether a substitute appears.
